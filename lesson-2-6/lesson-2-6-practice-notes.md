### Means, Sample Sizes, CI's

For the returns labelled Damaged, there was a sample size of 313, the mean = $100.73, and the CI was $92.73 to $108.74.

For the returns labelled Wrong Size, there was a sample size of 183, the mean = $82.36, and the CI was $70.78 to $93.94.  


### T-test p-value

From the t-test between Damaged and Wrong Size, p = 0.0104

### Memo for results

Damaged returns averaged $18.40 higher than Wrong Size returns, ($100.73 vs. $82.36; t = 2.57, p = 0.0104) The plausible range for the true difference is about $4 to $32. It is very unlikely that the difference is due to solely chance, but relative to the variation in each group it is moderate in size. This analysis cannot establish it's cause. 

### Observations from Histogram

From the histogram, there is a hard right skew and is unimodal, indicating that there are some high value outliers present impacting means. 

### Is it Practically Significant?

The result is indeed statistically significant, but falls into a bit of a gray area in terms of practical significance. It's clearly a non-zero difference and moderate compared to the mean, but is small when compared to the variation.

In regards to a home goods e-commerce company, when looking at the data per return, the difference is modest, but when you look at total dollars the gap appears larger due to the larger number of damaged returns. The gap likely reflects the types of products involved being more expensive or fragile when returned and less expensive or fragile when deemed wrong size.
